Every wave of productivity technology arrives with a promise that it will finally make teamwork better. Generative AI is the current one, and perhaps the last one.

With [Qing Xiao](https://qingxiao.info), [Xinlan Emily Hu](https://xehu.github.io), [Arvind Karunakaran](https://arvindkarunakaran.com), [Hong Shen](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~hongs/), and [Hancheng Cao](https://hanchengcao.me), we followed a project-based software development organization longitudinally from 2023 to 2025 — through the period in which generative AI tools went from novelty to default — to see what actually changed in how teams collaborated. The [preprint is on arXiv](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.10956).

The short version of what we found is in the title: AI hasn't fixed teamwork. It has, however, shifted the collaborative culture in measurable and sometimes surprising ways — in who does what, in what counts as a contribution, and in the texture of day-to-day coordination. That's a distinct finding from either "AI is transformative" or "AI changes nothing," and it lines up with a broader pattern in our [team research](/2025/09/01/task-space.html): the effects of a new tool depend on the task, the team, and the culture they arrive into.
